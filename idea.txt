=====================================================================================================================
SUPERCARS  -  THE GAME IDEA (full design document)
Author / brand: c0derz          Platform: iPhone (iOS 16+), landscape          Language: 100% native Swift
=====================================================================================================================

0. ONE-LINE PITCH
-----------------
An open-world supercar game for iPhone where you play as c0derz: drive a Porsche 992 GT3 R through a big living city
(day and night), crash into lamps and trees, race AI drivers, then park at your own neon-lit house, walk inside, sleep
in your bed, and tune the car in your garage (V6 / V8 / V10 / V12 / V16 engines, colours, rims, wing, tyres).
Everything (sounds, textures, city, animations) is generated by code or by our own pipeline; no web view, no game engine
other than Apple's own frameworks.


1. WHY THIS GAME EXISTS / DESIGN GOALS
--------------------------------------
- A game made only for one player: c0derz (the developer). Every screen carries the c0derz identity.
- Feel: arcade-simulation. Real-looking car and city, but forgiving and fun on a touch screen.
- Native and light: SceneKit (Metal) for 3D, SwiftUI for menus/HUD, AVAudioEngine for sound, CoreMotion for tilt steering.
- Free-roam first: there is no forced mission line. The player decides: drive, race, walk, sleep, tune.
- Unsigned IPA built on Bitrise (no Mac needed), installed with Sideloadly / AltStore / TrollStore.


2. THE C0DERZ IDENTITY (visual language)
----------------------------------------
- Colours: neon green #39FF88, magenta #FF2BD6, cyan #2BE6FF on near-black (#05060A) glass panels.
- Symbols: "</>" (code brackets), circuit-board traces, monospaced code text, "c0derz" in heavy neon lettering.
- Where it appears: logo on the menu ("SUPERCARS  </> by c0derz"), HUD colours, the house (murals, 3D neon letters on the roof,
  walls, garage), race gate banner ("SUPERCARS GP - c0derz"), monitor screens showing scrolling code, credits.


3. THE WORLD (open city)
------------------------
Size: roughly 3 km x 3 km, generated deterministically in code (same city every launch).
- Road network: a grid of streets (140 m pitch) with three classes: streets, avenues, and central boulevards with a planted
  median. A curved ring road circles downtown. A hairpin connector and a hillside suburb (Hillcrest Drive, Summit Lane and a
  cul-de-sac) complete the map.
- Districts (blocks):
    * Downtown: tall glass towers (up to about 215 m) with real-looking windows, mullions, reflections; ground-floor shops
      with signs and awnings; stepped tops, roof machinery, antennas with red beacons at night.
    * Mid-rise: the user's own building models (buildings.glb, 10 different buildings) plus generated low-rises with shops.
    * Residential: houses with gable roofs, chimneys, doors and garage doors.
    * Industrial: warehouses with roll-up doors.
    * Parks: lawn, a pond, many mango trees (the user's mango_tree.glb with 3 levels of detail), benches.
    * Plaza: paved square with a fountain, benches, ring of trees.
- Street detail: sidewalks, curbs, lane markings (dashed, solid, yellow centre lines), crosswalks and stop lines, manholes,
  street lamps with light pools on the road at night, traffic signals, road signs (stop, speed limit, street name, one way,
  no parking, yield, crossing, parking), benches.
- Destructible props: street lamps, road signs and trees are individually breakable.
    * Hit a lamp: it bends and falls, metal clang sound, dust; the collider disappears; the car loses some speed.
    * Hit a tree: crack, the tree topples slowly, leaves rustle, dust; bigger trees slow the car more.
    * Hit a sign: it flies/falls with a clang.
    * Buildings, walls and hedges are solid: the car bounces off, damage and sparks.
- Sky and time: full day/night cycle (default 24 real minutes per in-game day; adjustable). Sun and moon discs, stars at night,
  drifting clouds, twilight colours, fog matched to the horizon. Sun shadows (cascaded), city lights switch on at night:
  lit windows, lamp glow, shop signs, traffic lights, tower beacons.
- Performance design: static geometry is merged in 140 m chunks, chunks far from the player are hidden, trees swap between three
  detail levels by distance, draw distance and shadow quality are player settings.
- Race routes: "Downtown GP" (a rounded city circuit that starts at the race gate) and "Ring Road Sprint".
- Race gate: a big illuminated arch with a banner and a chequered start line on the road, plus a plaza area for racing.
- Minimap: circular, rotates with the car, shows roads, the race route and opponents.


4. THE CAR (Porsche 992 GT3 R)
------------------------------
- Model: user's 2024 Porsche 992 GT3 R, optimised for mobile; split into body, interior, glass, steering wheel, wheels,
  brakes and wings so each can move or be re-coloured. Interior has seat, roll cage, dashboard displays and pedals.
- Physics: our own arcade-simulation model (bicycle model with tyre slip, weight transfer, downforce, drivetrain and gearbox,
  traction control, ABS, stability assist). Ground surface changes grip (asphalt, concrete, sidewalk, grass, dirt).
- Engines (selectable in the garage, each has its own sound):
    V6 3.4L twin-turbo, V8 4.0L flat-plane, V10 5.2L screaming, V12 6.5L naturally aspirated, V16 8.0L quad-turbo.
    Different power, torque, weight, redline and price. V6 and V8 are owned at the start.
- Camera views (button cycles them): chase, close chase, hood, cockpit (inside the car, glass hidden), bumper.
    The classic bug "camera sometimes gets far from the car" is fixed by design: the follow camera has a hard maximum lag
    distance and snaps instantly after teleports.
- Cockpit view: the steering wheel turns with the player's input, and the driver's hands stay on the wheel (arm IK).
- Visual effects: skid marks, tyre smoke, sparks on scrapes, exhaust flames and pops when lifting off, damage smoke, headlights
  (real spot lights at night), brake lights, body pitch and roll, wheel spin and steering.
- Damage: crashes hurt the car (bar in the HUD); can be switched off in Settings.
- Customisation (garage): engine, body colour (16 presets + free colour picker), finish (gloss, metallic, matte, satin, chrome,
  pearl), race livery on/off, rim colour, brake caliper colour, window tint, rear wing (none / GT3 / big), tyres
  (road / sport / slick).


5. THE CHARACTER (c0derz)
-------------------------
- Model: user's rigged "bike rider 3d" avatar (68 bones). Full body is shown, on foot and in the car.
- Procedural animation (no animation clips exist, so everything is solved by code):
    idle (breathing, weight shift, looking around), walk, jog and sprint (planted feet, hip sway, arm swing, lean into turns,
    smooth blends), sitting in the car with both hands on the wheel and feet on the pedals, sleeping in bed, getting in/out.
- On foot: virtual joystick to move, drag to look, run button, interact button. Collisions with buildings, furniture and props.
- Third-person orbit camera that pulls in when a wall is behind it.


6. THE HOUSE AND GARAGE (home base)
-----------------------------------
Location: quiet hillside street (Summit Lane) at the edge of the city. Modern villa with an attached garage.
- Exterior: white plaster, dark cladding, wooden slat wall, canopy over the front door, decorative upper storey, flat roof with
  a magenta neon edge, lawn, hedges, driveway, planters, mango trees, neon posts at the driveway.
- c0derz design on the walls: painted murals (neon "c0derz", circuit-board traces, "</>"), big 3D neon "c0derz" letters on
  the roof, west wall, living room, bedroom, office and garage.
- Interior rooms: living room (sofa, TV that can be toggled, feature wall), entrance hall, kitchen (island, stools, fridge),
  bedroom (bed with LED headboard, nightstands, wardrobe), bathroom, home office (three monitors with scrolling code, gaming
  chair, server rack with blinking LEDs, circuit-board wall). Warm interior lights, fake outdoor views in the windows.
- Things you can do (interact button near them):
    Enter / leave the house through the front door; sleep in the bed (screen fades, time jumps to 07:00 of the next day, game is
    saved, day counter goes up); toggle the TV; do a freelance coding job at the desk (+$1,500, one minute cooldown);
    customise the car at the garage console.
- Garage: automatic roll-up door that opens when you or the car get close; epoxy floor with safety stripes, tool wall, tyre rack,
  lift posts, workbench, ceiling lights, neon mural. The car is parked inside for customisation; showroom lights and a neon
  ring switch on; the camera orbits the car (drag to rotate, pinch to zoom). Changes are shown live on the car.


7. RACING
---------
- Drive to the race gate and press the prompt: 3-2-1-GO countdown, 6-car grid (player + 5 AI), 3 laps.
- AI drivers follow the route with different skill (Settings: opponent skill), can be bumped, and finish with times.
- HUD during a race: position, lap, race time, lap time, best lap, wrong-way warning; results screen with times and prize money.
- Best laps, races, wins and distance are saved.


8. ECONOMY AND PROGRESSION
--------------------------
- Money is earned by racing (prize by position) and by freelance coding at the desk. It is spent in the garage on engines and
  tyres (colours, rims, wing, tint are free).
- TEST BUILD NOTE: money is currently practically unlimited ($999,999,999) and purchases are free, so everything can be tested.
  Before a real release: restore the starting money (25,000) and real spending in Core/SaveData.swift.
- Saved automatically (every 20 s and on pause / background): money, day, time of day, car setup, owned engines and tyres, records.


9. CONTROLS AND INPUT
---------------------
- Driving: tilt the phone like a steering wheel (calibrate button, live tilt meter), OR on-screen wheel, OR left/right buttons.
  Gas and brake pedals, handbrake, gear up/down, horn, headlights, camera-view button, pause.
- On foot: left joystick, right-side look drag, run toggle, jump button, big interact button; tapping the prompt also interacts.
- Left-handed layout, HUD/button size, control opacity, touch steering sensitivity, camera sensitivity, invert Y, haptics
  (crash, gear, UI vibrations).
- Game controllers (MFi, DualShock, DualSense, Xbox) and hardware keyboards are supported.


10. SETTINGS (everything the iPhone player can change)
------------------------------------------------------
- Graphics: preset (Low / Medium / High / Ultra / Custom), render resolution, shadow quality, anti-aliasing, FPS cap (30/60/120),
  draw distance, tree detail, prop density, reflections, bloom, HDR, motion blur, particles, camera shake.
- Controls: steering method, tilt sensitivity / full-lock angle / dead zone / smoothing, calibrate, touch steering, camera
  sensitivity, invert look, haptics, HUD scale, control opacity, left-handed, auto-throttle.
- Audio: master, engine and tyres, effects, music, ambience, test buttons.
- Gameplay: speed unit (km/h or mph), default camera, traction control, ABS, stability assist, crash damage, field of view,
  minimap, opponent skill, day length, reset to defaults.
- Credits: all model authors (see section 15).


11. UI FLOW
-----------
Loading screen (animated neon grid, progress bar, status text) -> Main menu over a slow cinematic orbit around the car
(Continue / New Game / Settings / Credits, stat card with money, engine, day, races, wins, distance, play time) -> game.
In game: HUD (clock, day, money, location, minimap, slim speedometer with RPM bar, gear, TC/ABS lights, damage bar, race panel,
interaction prompt, toasts), pause menu (resume, settings, reset car to the street, save and quit), garage screen, race results,
sleep fade with "Zzz".


12. SOUND (all synthesised, no samples, no copyrighted audio)
------------------------------------------------------------
Generated by Python (numpy/scipy) and encoded to AAC (.m4a), about 7 MB in total, 93 files:
- Engines: five engine types x five loops (idle, low, mid, high, decel), cross-faded and pitch-shifted by RPM.
- Car: engine start/stop, exhaust pops, gear shift, turbo whoosh, horn, headlight click, tyre skid (asphalt and grass), kerb rumble,
  gravel, wind and road noise, metal crashes (light and heavy), glass, scrape, lamp bend/fall, tree crack/fall, leaves, sign clang,
  debris, doors, seatbelt.
- On foot and house: footsteps (concrete, grass, wood), doors, garage door motor, lift motor, bed rustle, sleep chime, light
  switch, cash register, wrench ratchet.
- UI and race: taps, confirm, back, swipe, error, toggles, countdown beep, GO, lap complete, win fanfare, lose, purchase.
- Ambience loops: city day/night, suburb day/night, house interior, garage interior.
- Music (16-bar synth tracks): menu, drive, race, garage, night.
- Positional 3D sound for world effects, stereo music/ambience with cross-fades.


13. TECHNOLOGY
--------------
- Swift 5.9 with SceneKit (PBR materials, cascaded sun shadows, HDR + bloom), SwiftUI overlays, AVAudioEngine, CoreMotion,
  GameController, CoreHaptics/feedback generators. No third-party packages.
- Our own glTF/GLB loader written in Swift (iOS cannot read GLB): skins, PBR materials, embedded images, texture size limits.
- Procedural textures (asphalt, facades with lit-window maps, pavers, roof tiles, wood floor, murals, code screens) drawn with
  CoreGraphics at launch.
- Structure: Core (frame loop, settings, save, camera), Assets, World, Vehicle, Character, House, Audio, Input, UI.
- Build: XcodeGen project.yml (plus a committed fallback Xcode project), Bitrise workflow (bitrise.yaml) that archives an UNSIGNED
  IPA. Static checkers (tools/check_swift.py, check_calls.py, check_members.py) exist because the code is written on Windows.


14. ASSET PIPELINE (where Blender fits)
---------------------------------------
- Original models supplied by c0derz live in assets_src/ (Porsche 992 GT3 R, bike rider 3d, Buildings, Mango Tree).
- Python scripts (tools/asset_*.py) split and optimise them for mobile: decimation of the heavy meshes through the Blender 2.79
  MCP bridge (C:\blender-claude), texture downsizing, correct scale, named parts (wheels, steering wheel, glass, ...), 3 tree
  levels of detail, 10 separate building files, JSON metadata (car anchors, building sizes, rider bones).
- The Blender street map made earlier for the Python racer is NOT used in the iPhone game; the city is generated in Swift.
  (Possible improvement: import that Blender street as an extra district.)

17. KNOWN ISSUES / THINGS TO VERIFY
-----------------------------------
- Character animation is fully procedural and was never seen by the developer before user testing: check feet, arms, sitting pose,
  hands on the wheel (arm reach adapts by leaning), getting in and out of the car.
- Driver seat and steering-wheel anchors in car_meta.json were estimated from the model; may need small nudges.
- City draw-call cost may be high on old iPhones (use Low/Medium presets); tree and chunk counts can be reduced.
- Sounds were designed numerically and never listened to by the author: levels and character may need tuning.
- The house has one storey inside (upper volume is decorative); no stairs.
- Traffic and pedestrians do not exist yet.
- The build uses SceneKit, which Apple has marked as legacy: it works on current iOS but future migration to RealityKit/Metal
  could be considered.

19. HOW TO CONTINUE WORKING ON IT
---------------------------------
1. Edit the Swift files (or ask Claude to), keep the folder structure.
2. Run: python tools/gen_xcodeproj.py  (only when adding or removing .swift files)
3. Run: python tools/check_swift.py --symbols  and  python tools/check_calls.py  for a quick static check.
4. Upload: python tools/upload_to_github.py --skip-assets-src   (needs a GitHub token with the "repo" scope)
5. Bitrise: run the workflow build_ipa; download Supercars.ipa from Artifacts; install with Sideloadly / AltStore / TrollStore.
6. If the build fails, copy only the lines that contain "error:" from the log and give them to Claude.
7. Regenerate assets or sounds when needed: python tools/optimize_assets.py (needs the Blender MCP bridge for decimation),
   python tools/make_audio_all.py (needs numpy, scipy, ffmpeg).


20. LIVING CITY UPDATE (added after the first version of this document)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Premium look: the neon green / magenta / cyan identity is gone. Menus, HUD, map, house art and the race gate now use graphite, white,
platinum and one muted brass accent; no glow, no gradients on borders. The c0derz signature stays, small: brass lettering on the house,
a plaque at the door, "c0derz" under the SUPERCARS wordmark.

Endless world: the 3 km city island repeats in every direction. Between two islands there is a green belt with streets and street lamps.
Chunks outside the first island are streamed clones of a source chunk (shared geometry, their own props, trees and colliders) and are
released again behind the player. The ground plane follows the player. Surfaces, road queries, pedestrian and traffic routes all wrap
into the base tile. The hillside suburb with the player's house exists once per island.

Pedestrians: six archetypes (Christie, Jamal, Peter, the man in the suit, the casual woman, the rider), each with tinted shirt, trousers,
shoes, hair and hat colours from muted palettes, a body scale of +-4 %, and a personality (calm, normal, energetic, slow walker, tourist,
worker, business) that changes speed, restlessness, curiosity and phone use. The models have no animation clips, so the skeleton is
posed procedurally: the gait phase advances by speed divided by stride length and the planted foot moves back at exactly body speed, so
feet do not slide and speed always matches the animation. Speeds: stroll 1.1-1.7 m/s, brisk 1.7-2.3, run 2.5-4.5 (the player too).
Behaviour: choose a destination (shop window, roadside, park, plaza, taxi stand, home), walk on the sidewalk graph, wait at the curb until
no vehicle is coming, cross, stand, look around, check the phone or watch, talk to a neighbour, react to fast cars and crashes, get bumped.
Population follows the hour and the district and is limited by the graphics preset; simulation is level-of-detail based (near / mid /
far / dormant) with pooled characters and no allocation while playing.

Taxis: cruise, spot somebody at a taxi stand or a pedestrian who raises a hand, stop at the curb, the passenger walks to the rear right
door, turns, bends and sits down, the taxi drives to a destination at least 250 m away, stops at the curb, the passenger steps out and
walks on. The AI drives with acceleration, braking, steering smoothing, lane following, first-come-first-served intersections and it
stops for people and cars.

Police and wanted level: 0-5 stars. Reckless events (hard crashes, hitting people, ramming police, speeding or driving off-road in sight
of a patrol) add heat; heat decays when the player is out of sight. 1 star: one unit investigates. 2 stars: chase with siren. 3+ stars:
more units and higher speed limits. Stop next to a police car for a few seconds to be let off with a warning.

Map and navigation: full-screen map (button under the minimap), pan, pinch zoom, tap a place or drop a pin, set a destination, route line
on the map and the minimap, compass arrow and distance on the HUD, light column over a far destination. A new game starts with the race
already marked. Places: Home, Garage, Race Start, Race Finish (paddock), Police Station, six taxi stands, districts.

Race place: a real complex on the road in front of the plaza: starting grid, barriers, sponsor boards, marshal tower, grandstand, flags,
direction signs at the approaches and starting lights over the gate.

City extras: police station with car park and parked police trucks in the civic block, taxi stands, two mini roundabouts, fire hydrants
and litter bins.

Car: four independent spring / damper corners (load transfer from braking, acceleration and cornering, aero downforce, kerbs and rough
ground), Ackermann steering, wheel rotation from real wheel speed (front wheels see the steering angle, inner wheels of a turn turn
slower, locked brakes stop the wheel, wheelspin adds), kerb climbing on sidewalks, camera that pulls back under acceleration and comes
in under braking. The garage problem (the car only moved with full throttle) was the surface model: the whole plot counted as grass.

House: no more grass blanket. Gravel, pavers, planting beds, shrubs, one small lawn patch, bollard lights. Interior: the gaming chair
model sits at a real desk setup in the office; bookshelf, curtains, framed prints, pendant lamps, rugs in calm colours.

Developer tools (hidden): DEBUG builds always; release builds after seven taps on the SUPERCARS wordmark. Wanted level, teleports, car
repair / reset, money, unlock all, time of day, weather (clear, overcast, fog, rain), toggle pedestrians / taxis / police, spawn units.

Never compiled on the authoring machine: treat the first Bitrise run as a compile-fix round. Everything that moves (pedestrians, cars,
suspension, map) is untested on a device.
